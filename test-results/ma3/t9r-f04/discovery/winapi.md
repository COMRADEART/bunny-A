# Windows Job Object semantics: security-gate research report

Sources are Microsoft Learn pages fetched or searched during this pass. Raymond Chen's Old New Thing posts were not consulted. Where a claim is inference or from general knowledge rather than a quoted doc, it is marked **[inference]** or **[unverified]**.

## 1. Nested jobs and breakaway

**Short answer.** A child joins every job in its parent's job chain by default. Breakaway is decided by the immediate job first. If the immediate job does not permit breakaway, nothing above it is escaped. Escaping an inner job does not automatically leave a non-breakaway ancestor.

**Doc rules:**
- Nested Jobs, "Creating a Nested Job Hierarchy": "When processes are implicitly associated with a job during process creation, a child process is associated with every job in the job chain of its parent process. If the immediate job object allows breakaway, the child process breaks away from the immediate job object and from each job in the parent job chain, moving up the hierarchy until it reaches a job that does not permit breakaway. If the immediate job object does not allow breakaway, the child process does not break away even if jobs in its parent job chain allow it." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/nested-jobs#creating-a-nested-job-hierarchy)
- AssignProcessToJobObject, Remarks: "The child process breaks away from every job in the job chain unless a job in the chain does not allow breakaway. In this case, the child process does not break away from that job or any job above it in the job chain." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/jobapi2/nf-jobapi2-assignprocesstojobobject#remarks)
- CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB (Process Creation Flags): "If the calling process is associated with a job, the job must set the JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_BREAKAWAY_OK limit." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/process-creation-flags)

**Case A: outer P has BREAKAWAY_OK or SILENT_BREAKAWAY_OK, inner J has neither.** A descendant of a J member has J as its immediate job. J does not permit breakaway, so the child does not break away from J or anything above it. Implicit escape is blocked. CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB also fails, because the immediate job (J) must set BREAKAWAY_OK. Explicit escape by AssignProcessToJobObject into a different job is governed by the hierarchy rules, so it is not a free escape.

**Case B: inner J has no breakaway flags, a further-nested job K created by a J member has BREAKAWAY_OK | SILENT_BREAKAWAY_OK | KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE (the libuv pattern).** The child of a K member has K as immediate job. K permits breakaway, so the child moves up. It leaves K, then checks J. J does not permit breakaway, so the process stops there. **The child remains in J.** The breakaway from K does not leave J. This follows directly from the "moving up the hierarchy until it reaches a job that does not permit breakaway" rule. Doc quotes are the same as above; the conclusion about K and J is a reading of them **[inference from the quoted rule]**.

## 2. PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_JOB_LIST

- UpdateProcThreadAttribute: "PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_JOB_LIST: The lpValue parameter is a pointer to a list of job handles to be assigned to the child process, in the order specified. Supported in Windows 10 and newer and Windows Server 2016 and newer." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-updateprocthreadattribute#parameters)
- The value "must persist until the attribute list is destroyed using the DeleteProcThreadAttributeList function." Same source.
- Ordering: the docs say only "in the order specified." Nothing further is documented on the meaning of order for nested jobs.
- Attribute list use: "To specify these attributes when creating a process, specify EXTENDED_STARTUPINFO_PRESENT." (InitializeProcThreadAttributeList, Remarks: https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-initializeprocthreadattributelist#remarks)
- **Gap:** the fetched docs do not state the behavior when the creating process is itself in a job, nor the specific error codes when a job assignment is refused. **[unverified]** Expected behavior is that the assignment rules of AssignProcessToJobObject apply (ERROR_ACCESS_DENIED for a refused assignment is typical), and CreateProcess fails with no child created. Confirm against a live test before relying on it.

## 3. JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE

- Fires when the last job handle is closed: "However, if the job has the JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE flag specified, closing the last job object handle terminates all associated processes and then destroys the job object itself. If a nested job has the JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_KILL_ON_JOB_CLOSE flag specified, closing the last job object handle terminates all processes associated with the job and its child jobs in the hierarchy." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/job-objects#managing-job-objects)
- "The job is destroyed when its last handle has been closed and all associated processes have been terminated." Same source.
- **Duplicated handles keep the job alive.** The doc counts every open job handle, so a duplicate in another process prevents the kill from firing. Kill-on-close fires only when the last handle of any kind closes. This follows from the quoted rule plus the DuplicateHandle doc, which says "any changes to the object are reflected through both handles" and that duplication "ensures that the reference count is increased so that the mutex object will not be destroyed until both threads have closed the handle" (DuplicateHandle example, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/handleapi/nf-handleapi-duplicatehandle). The example is for mutexes, but the rule is generic **[inference for jobs]**.
- DuplicateHandle source requirements: "The handle must have the PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE access right" on hSourceProcessHandle. The target process handle "must have the PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE access right." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/handleapi/nf-handleapi-duplicatehandle)
- Reduced access: dwDesiredAccess specifies the new handle's access. It is ignored if DUPLICATE_SAME_ACCESS is set. "In some cases, the new handle can have more access rights than the original handle. However, in other cases, DuplicateHandle cannot create a handle with more access rights than the original." So a reduced-access duplicate is possible. Whether a job handle with only query access still counts toward kill-on-close is not stated in the docs; it counts as a handle **[inference]**.
- Job access rights (Job Object Security and Access Rights, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/job-object-security-and-access-rights):
  - Assign to job via JOB_LIST / AssignProcessToJobObject: **JOB_OBJECT_ASSIGN_PROCESS (0x0001)**. The process handle also needs PROCESS_SET_QUOTA and PROCESS_TERMINATE (AssignProcessToJobObject doc).
  - TerminateJobObject: **JOB_OBJECT_TERMINATE (0x0008)**.
  - QueryInformationJobObject / IsProcessInJob: **JOB_OBJECT_QUERY (0x0004)**.
  - SetInformationJobObject: **JOB_OBJECT_SET_ATTRIBUTES (0x0002)**.
- "The handle returned by CreateJobObject has JOB_OBJECT_ALL_ACCESS access... If a job object is in a hierarchy of nested jobs, a caller with access to the job object implicitly has access to all of its child jobs in the hierarchy." (same page). This is important for the gate: access to a parent job implies access to its children.

## 4. JOBOBJECT_BASIC_ACCOUNTING_INFORMATION (ActiveProcesses / TotalProcesses)

- **TotalProcesses:** "The total number of processes associated with the job during its lifetime, including those that have terminated. For example, when a process is associated with a job, but the association fails because of a limit violation, this value is incremented." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/desktop/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_accounting_information)
- **ActiveProcesses:** "The total number of processes currently associated with the job. When a process is associated with a job, but the association fails because of a limit violation, this value is temporarily incremented. When the terminated process exits and all references to the process are released, this value is decremented." (same source)
- The SetInformationJobObject / JOBOBJECT_BASIC_LIMIT_INFORMATION remarks say the count is incremented on association attempt and decremented only when the process terminates and all its handles are closed. An open handle to a dead process keeps ActiveProcesses high. (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/winnt/ns-winnt-jobobject_basic_limit_information#remarks)
- **Nested jobs:** "Resource accounting information for a nested job describes the usage of every process associated with that job, including processes in child jobs. Each job in a job chain therefore represents the aggregated resources used by its own processes and the processes of every child job below it in the job chain." (Nested Jobs, Resource Accounting; https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/nested-jobs). Also: "accounting information for each child job is aggregated in its parent job" (Job Objects, Resource Accounting). **So yes, processes in nested child jobs are counted in the parent's ActiveProcesses and TotalProcesses.** The exact field-level wording is inferred from the aggregation statements **[inference for the field]**.

## 5. Ways code can run outside a job without breakaway

Official sources:
- **Win32_Process.Create.** The Job Objects page says: "Child processes created using Win32_Process.Create are not associated with the job." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/job-objects#managing-processes-in-jobs). **Conflict:** the Win32_Process.Create Remarks say: "Processes created with the Win32_Process.Create method are limited by the job object unless the CREATE_BREAKAWAY_FROM_JOB flag is specified." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/cimwin32prov/create-method-in-class-win32-process#return-value). **Treat WMI Create as an escape route until tested.** WMI runs in the WMI provider host, not as a child of the caller **[inference]**.
- **Scheduled tasks.** "Processes started by Win32_ScheduledJob.Create run under the LocalSystem account that can confer too much privilege." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/wmisdk/creating-processes-remotely). The Task Scheduler service is not a descendant of the caller, so it is outside the job **[inference]**.
- **COM out-of-process and DllSurrogate.** The COM activation path launches a surrogate (dllhost.exe) under the DCOM Service Control Manager, not as a child of the client (https://learn.microsoft.com/troubleshoot/sql/database-engine/development/run-dll-based-com-object; https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/com/registering-the-dll-server-for-surrogate-activation). **Whether the surrogate is in the client's job is not stated** **[inference: no]**. LocalServer32 EXE servers are "always launched in preference" to surrogates (same source).
- **Services, ShellExecute to explorer.gui, WSL/wsl.exe VM, RunAs / CreateProcessWithLogonW, CreateProcessWithTokenW, CreateProcessAsUser, UWP activation broker.** The fetched sources do not address job membership for these. CreateProcessWithTokenW and CreateProcessWithLogonW are documented as creating processes with another security context (Process and Thread Functions, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/process-and-thread-functions#process-functions), but job membership is not stated. **[unverified]** Treat each as an escape route until tested, because the process is created by a service or broker rather than as a child of the job member.

## 6. Process DACL hardening (deny PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE / PROCESS_VM_WRITE to same-user processes)

- A process can call SetSecurityInfo on its own process object: "To change a process's security descriptor, call the SetSecurityInfo function." (Process Security and Access Rights, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/procthread/process-security-and-access-rights). SetSecurityInfo supports "Processes, threads, jobs, and file-mapping objects" (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/aclapi/nf-aclapi-setsecurityinfo#remarks).
- Denying specific rights is possible with an access-denied ACE placed first: "The user's access-denied ACE must appear first; otherwise... it will grant access" (DACLs and ACEs, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/secauthz/dacls-and-aces).
- **Limitation (the doc's own warning):** "A process that has some of the access rights noted here can use them to gain other access rights. For example, if process A has a handle to process B with PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE access, it can duplicate the pseudo handle for process B." Any process that can obtain a handle with PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE to the target can escalate. A DACL hardening that denies same-user processes is only as strong as the handles those processes already hold.
- **Owner rights:** the fetched docs say nothing about the OWNER RIGHTS SID or implicit owner access to a process DACL. **[unverified]** From general knowledge, an owner gets implicit READ_CONTROL and WRITE_DAC unless an OWNER RIGHTS ACE restricts it. Verify before relying on it.
- **SeDebugPrivilege:** "To open a handle to another process and obtain full access rights, you must enable the SeDebugPrivilege privilege." (Process Security and Access Rights). The doc does not explicitly say it bypasses the DACL. **[unverified]**. Assume a debug-privileged same-user admin process can open the target regardless of the DACL.
- **Official hardening options found:**
  - Protected processes deny PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE, PROCESS_VM_WRITE, and related rights to callers: "The following specific access rights are not allowed from a process to a protected process: ... PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE ... PROCESS_VM_WRITE" (Process Security and Access Rights, Protected Processes). Requires the platform protected-process mechanism, not a DACL.
  - PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_CHILD_PROCESS_POLICY with PROCESS_CREATION_CHILD_PROCESS_RESTRICTED: "this restriction is only effective in sandboxed applications (such as AppContainer) which ensure privileged process handles are not accessible to the process. For example, if a process restricting child process creation is able to access another process handle with PROCESS_CREATE_PROCESS or PROCESS_VM_WRITE access rights, then it may be possible to bypass the child process restriction." (UpdateProcThreadAttribute, Remarks, https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/processthreadsapi/nf-processthreadsapi-updateprocthreadattribute#remarks). This is the most direct official statement that a DACL-only model is insufficient.

## 7. IsProcessInJob with nested jobs

- The doc says: "JobHandle: A handle to the job. If this parameter is NULL, the function tests if the process is running under any job." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/jobapi/nf-jobapi-isprocessinjob). It does not explicitly address nested jobs.
- Given the nesting rule that a child process "is associated with every job in the job chain of its parent process" (Nested Jobs), a process in a child job is also associated with J. **So IsProcessInJob(p, J) should return TRUE for a process in a job nested under J** **[inference from the nesting rule]**. Confirm with a live test.
- Requires JOB_OBJECT_QUERY on the job handle and PROCESS_QUERY_LIMITED_INFORMATION on the process handle (IsProcessInJob doc).

## 8. QueryInformationJobObject with hJob = NULL

- "If this value is NULL and the calling process is associated with a job, the job associated with the calling process is used. If the job is nested, the immediate job of the calling process is used." (https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/api/jobapi2/nf-jobapi2-queryinformationjobobject#parameters)
- **Answer:** it returns the immediate (innermost) job of the caller, not the outermost. Corollary: the NULL form does not expose the parents. **Parents need a handle with JOB_OBJECT_QUERY** **[inference]**.
- The IsProcessInJob remarks say: "an application can call the QueryInformationJobObject function with NULL to obtain information about the job object." (IsProcessInJob, Remarks). This is information-only, so the caller does not gain a handle that could be used to terminate or reconfigure the job.

## Implications for design

- **Do not treat a single job as an enforcement boundary against a same-user process.** Escape paths include WMI Create (conflicting docs), scheduled tasks, COM/DCOM surrogates, services, and possibly the shell, RunAs, WSL, and UWP broker. Each needs a test before it is claimed as blocked.
- **Breakaway flags decide escape per job, and the immediate job decides first.** Set neither BREAKAWAY_OK nor SILENT_BREAKAWAY_OK on the innermost job that must be contained. A member of that job then cannot escape it, even if outer or inner-sibling jobs permit breakaway (Case A). Also check that nested sub-jobs created by members (e.g., libuv's job) do not reach past the contained job (Case B says they cannot).
- **Assume a child of a permissive job can still create a process in a different job** only if the immediate job permits it. Test the Case B chain on the target Windows version rather than relying on the reading here.
- **Kill-on-close is not a reliable containment guarantee** while any duplicated job handle exists. Any process holding a duplicate keeps the job alive. Use TerminateJobObject for an explicit kill, and audit who holds job handles (PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE on the holder is the escape vector).
- **Grant job rights minimally.** Gate each use by right: ASSIGN_PROCESS (0x1), SET_ATTRIBUTES (0x2), QUERY (0x4), TERMINATE (0x8). Remember that access to a parent job implicitly grants access to all its children.
- **Accounting counts nested processes in parents.** Use ActiveProcesses on the outer job to detect any descendant, but note that dead processes with open handles keep ActiveProcesses high.
- **PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_JOB_LIST is Windows 10+ only.** Design a fallback for older targets, and verify the error behavior and assignment order on a real machine (not covered by the docs fetched here).
- **DACL hardening on the process object is not sufficient.** Any process holding PROCESS_DUP_HANDLE on the target can duplicate its way to full access. Use protected-process status or the child-process-restriction policy inside an AppContainer, and treat SeDebugPrivilege holders as out of scope unless proven otherwise.
- **Open items before the gate is final:** verify (a) Case B on the target Windows version, (b) WMI Win32_Process.Create job membership, (c) JOB_LIST error codes and ordering, (d) IsProcessInJob with nested jobs, (e) OWNER RIGHTS and SeDebugPrivilege DACL behavior, and (f) the job membership of COM surrogates, services, RunAs, WSL, and UWP-launched processes.
